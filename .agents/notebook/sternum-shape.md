# Sternum Shape & Geometric Topology

## Shape Overview & Analogy
- **Turtle Plastron**: The sternum forms a bilateral, shield-like ventral breastplate (analogous to a turtle's plastron). It anchors the prosoma underneath and serves as the articulation bed for leg coxae.
- **Orientation & Coordinate Frame**:
  - **Sagittal Symmetry**: Symmetrical across the midline plane (`X = 0`).
  - **Longitudinal Axis (Z)**: Negative Z is anterior (front / labium); positive Z is posterior (back / pedicel).
  - **Vertical Depth (Y)**: Negative Y is descended / ventral concavity; Y = 0 defines the neutral outer rim plane.

## Silhouette & Outer Rim
- **Truncated Anterior**: A flat or narrow front margin spanning across the midline to meet the mouthparts.
- **Widest Span**: Reaches maximum lateral half-width at the center line (`Z = 0`).
- **Posterior Taper**: Curves smoothly back and inward from the lateral apex toward a pointed posterior terminal at the rear apex.
- **Segmented Boundary**: The rim is segmented into distinct edge intervals with midpoints, providing dedicated socket boundaries for each leg's coxa base.

## Central Spine & Ventral Depth
- **Descended Keel**: A central longitudinal spine runs along `X = 0` from the anterior margin to the posterior apex.
- **Ventral Hollowing**: Rather than a flat planar sheet, the central spine descends downward along `-Y`, giving the plastron a shallow, hollow boat-keel / bowl cross-section.
- **Longitudinal Easing**: Descent reaches its maximum depth near the widest lateral span (`Z = 0`) and smoothly eases upward toward both the anterior edge and posterior apex.

## Face Topology
- **Bilateral Quad Strip**: The surface is predominantly composed of paired quads arranged side-by-side, spanning laterally from the central spine outward to the corresponding rim segments.
- **Terminal Diamond Quad**: At the rear apex convergence, the bilateral quad rows merge into a single symmetrical 4-point diamond (kite) quad across the midline, rather than splitting into two adjacent quads or pinching into triangles.

## Concentric Loops
The sternum topology incorporates three concentric loops around its body:
1. **Inner Transition Loop (1 Inset Loop)**:
   - Inset inward across all base faces between the central spine and outer rim, with its vertices elevated intermediate in Y (between the lowest spine depth and the rim plane).
   - **Purpose**: Breaks the cross-sectional profile from spine to rim into a smooth curvature, preventing a sharp linear V-groove.
2. **Outer Support Loops (2 Outset Loops)**:
   - Two concentric rings extruded outward from the perimeter rim.
   - **Purpose**: Create structural support and flexible membrane buffer rings surrounding the core rigid plate, serving as connective tissue interfacing with coxa bases and the dorsal carapace.

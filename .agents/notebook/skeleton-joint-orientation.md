# Skeleton Joint Orientation & Yaw Distortion in Skin Deform

## Symptom
`deform_skin` (`kinefx::jointdeform`) inside the `skeleton` subnet exhibits noticeable yaw (out-of-plane horizontal displacement) on the spider legs, most prominently at the trochanter–femur and femur–patella joints, even though the source geometry generated by `/obj/spider/geometry` lies strictly in a vertical plane with zero yaw.

## Root Cause Analysis

### 1. Geometry & Rest Skeleton are Planar
- In `/obj/spider/geometry`, leg segments are constructed with their centerlines on $X = 0$ in segment space and rotated into place by a horizontal rotation around world $Y$.
- All bone joint positions (`coxa`, `trochanter`, `femur`, `patella`, `tibia`, `metatarsus`, `tarsus`, `tip`) for each leg lie strictly in a single vertical plane defined by direction vector $D_{xz}$ and world up $(0, 1, 0)$. The plane normal is $N = (-D_z, 0, D_x)$.
- Rest positions have zero yaw offset ($0.0$ distance from the plane).

### 2. Naive Shortest-Arc Alignment in `rig_doctor`
- Joint orientations are initialized in `_add_rig_doctor` via:
  ```python
  rd.parm("inittransforms").set(1)
  rd.parm("reorienttochild").set(1)
  ```
- `inittransforms` sets transforms to identity (`X = [1,0,0], Y = [0,1,0], Z = [0,0,1]`).
- `reorienttochild` performs a simple `dihedral([0, 0, 1], z_aim)` to point local $+Z$ towards the next joint.
- Because leg segments have non-zero vertical slopes ($\Delta Y \ne 0$ from joint offsets, socket heights, and segment wedging):
  - The rotation axis of the dihedral is $[0, 0, 1] \times Z_{\text{aim}} = [Z_y, -Z_x, 0]$.
  - Because $Z_y \ne 0$, this axis is tilted relative to world $Y$.
  - Rotating world $X$ around this tilted axis causes the resulting local $X$ axis (`Row 0` of `transform`) to acquire a significant non-zero $Y$ component ($X_y \ne 0$).
  - For example on leg R1:
    - `coxa`: $X_y = +0.0726$
    - `trochanter`: $X_y = +0.0326$
    - `femur`: $X_y = +0.1053$
    - `patella`: $X_y = -0.1553$
    - `tibia`: $X_y = +0.0417$
- Consequently, local $X$ is **not** parallel to the leg's plane normal $N$ ($Y = 0$), and consecutive joints have mutually twisted, misaligned local frames.

### 3. Large Flex Rotations in `rig_pose` Induce Yaw
- `_add_rig_pose` poses the joints to the midpoint of their flexion ranges:
  ```python
  rx = 180.0 - 0.5 * (lo_flex + hi_flex)
  rp.parmTuple(f"r{index}").set((rx, ry, 0.0))
  ```
- For `femur`, $rx = -55.0^\circ$. For `patella`, $rx = +85.0^\circ$.
- Because `rig_pose` applies $rx$ around the joint's tilted local $X$ axis, rotating around an axis with a non-zero $Y$ component introduces a large horizontal component (yaw rotation in the local XZ plane).
- Over large segment lengths, this swings the patella and tibia tens of millimeters out of plane:
  - Femur center displacement: $+18.9\text{ mm}$
  - Patella center displacement: $+50.5\text{ mm}$
- `kinefx::jointdeform` applies this relative transform to the skin mesh, producing the obvious yaw distortion.

## Resolution
To eliminate yaw while preserving pure flexion in the vertical plane:
- Joint orientations must be computed with an explicit planar normal:
  - Local $X$ set to the leg plane normal $N = (-D_z, 0, D_x)$ (horizontal, $Y = 0$).
  - Local $Z$ set to the normalized child direction $Z_{\text{aim}}$.
  - Local $Y$ set to $Z_{\text{aim}} \times X$.
- When local $X$ is strictly perpendicular to the vertical plane, $rx$ rotations produce pure planar pitch with zero yaw distortion ($< 0.008\text{ mm}$ out-of-plane deviation).
